# BUILD REPORT v0.55 — the charter gets an instrument, and Jerry's premise was wrong

Shipped as **v0.55**, tagged `v0.55`. `VERSION`, the footer and the tag agree.

The first round under STANDING-RULINGS §16, the parity charter: *the source is the balance
authority; the simulator is an instrument.* Ten parts, five cumulative prefixes, **24 live
suites, 1,167 assertions, 0 failures**, and five 2,500-year pacing runs.

**The headline is Part 0.1, and it is a directive whose stated premise is false.**

> Jerry: *"Kitten's farmers are affected by seasonality."*
>
> **They are not.** `js/village.js updateResourceProduction()` applies skill, rank, leader and
> happiness to job output and nothing else. `getWeatherMod()` lives in `js/calendar.js` and is
> applied to the catnip **field**, never to the job path. The Kittens wiki's Game Mechanics page
> says it in one sentence: *"Seasons affect catnip production from catnip fields, but do not
> affect production from farmers."*
>
> **The directive shipped anyway** — directives override the spec, and this one is a good change
> on its own merits: Deepwinter now cuts the settlement's entire job-based food supply by 75%
> for a quarter of every year, which is what winter has always advertised and never delivered.
> What it does *not* get to be is PARITY. It is recorded as **RR-ORIGINAL, HARDER** — the first
> HARDER label the charter has produced, and the one that proves the labelling machinery does
> something other than rubber-stamp.

**And the round's most informative failure is the opposite of that:** the spec predicted the
Part 7/8 slice would move pacing by **≈ 0** because "neither is a pacing item." It moved Era 3
by **−193.6 game-years**. Doubling wanderer experience is the single largest accelerant this
round shipped, and it was classified as flavour.

## 1. My errors, first

1. **I let the spec's own prediction table set my expectations for s5, and did not sanity-check
   it before running.** "XP + undo — neither is expected to move pacing" is wrong on its face
   once stated plainly: wanderer rank is a *production multiplier*, per trade, and Part 7
   doubles the rate at which every wanderer climbs it. The median XP bank at Icathia went
   46,905 → 176,750 (×3.77). I should have flagged this at read time and instrumented for it
   deliberately rather than discovering it in a diff.

2. **My first `test-v55` camp assertion claimed Σ 5.10 for a probe that measured Σ 5.20**, because
   I added the seven mapped members and the RR-original Trailblazer trait together and then
   labelled the total "Kittens' own." Caught by the assertion failing, not by review. The suite
   now separates the two: **Σ 5.10 for the seven mapped members**, and the Trailblazer reported
   separately as an eighth, RR-original, hard-bounded term.

3. **Two `test-v55` probes compared a rounded reference against an unrounded call** and read as
   "the value moved." That is a false-positive generator in exactly the assertions meant to
   prove a term was *removed* — the worst place to have one.

4. **Eleven shipped suites broke this round**, more than any round since v0.47. All eleven are
   re-pointed in §7 with a superseding spec item; **none was deleted to make a number green**,
   and two of them (`test-offline-v54`'s two parity checks) turned out to be reporting a
   property of the *fixture* rather than of the game — see §5.

## 4. Part 3 — the food economy, and the one-tenth scale nobody had noticed

RR's food ledger was running at **exactly one-tenth of Kittens'**, and it was the *only* resource
on that scale. Kittens' farmer is `catnipPerTickBase 0.5` = 2.5/s at 5 ticks/s… against RR's
0.5/s. Every other job rate in the game was already 1:1 with the source; this one was 1:10.

That is not a balance opinion, it is a units error, and it had been compounding since v0.44
because every provisions figure derived from it inherited the same scale.

**`PROVISIONS_SCALE = 10`, and the sweep was enumerated rather than grepped.** Thirteen sites:

```
baseCap            200 → 2,000      storehouse caps    750 → 7,500
longhouse          120 → 1,200      harbor caps      1,000 → 10,000
bardsHearth         40 → 400        caravanserai       300 → 3,000
trainingGround      50 → 500        pastureRotation    800 → 8,000
abyssJourney        50 → 500        drakeHunt        4,00 → 4,000
voidExpedition     200 → 2,000      baron            2,000 → 20,000
festivalCost     6×pop → 60×pop     arrival threshold    8 → 80  (two call sites)
```

The spec offered a fallback if the sweep proved wide. **It did not — it is thirteen sites**, so
the full ×10 shipped. `test-v55` asserts the enumeration itself, not a spot check: every
provisions cap and cost in `BUILDINGS`, `UPGRADES`, `TECHS`, `EXPEDITIONS`, `FACTIONS`, `CRAFTS`
and `WTECHS` is read out of the live game and checked against the old value × 10.

**Farmer 5.000/s. Farmstead 0.625/s** — Kittens' `field` is `catnipPerTickBase 0.125`, and RR
had shipped 0.14, which at RR's own tenth-scale was **×2.24 the source**. The starter food
building was the largest un-flagged EASIER item in the food economy.

### The Granary, and re-ruling `eatCutLimit` from measurement

Kittens has **two** pastures and RR had ported one. `pasture` (catnip 100 + wood 10, ratio 1.15,
`catnipDemandRatio -0.005`) ships as the **Granary** on `logistics` — RR's 500 rung, which is
where Kittens' `animal` sits, and the better role match of the two 500s. `auditRawGraph()` 0.

The bound needed a ruling because two members now share it. Measured before setting it:

| granaries + pastures | Σ | delivered | reading |
|---|---|---|---|
| 40 + 30 | 0.290 | **29.000%** | fully linear — the bound does not bite |
| 60 + 40 | 0.420 | **40.809%** | bending |
| 500 + 500 | 4.000 | **49.583%** | asymptote |

**Ruling: keep 0.5.** It is a tail-cap, not a tax. At every count a player will actually reach
it is linear; it only bends where the stack would otherwise run away. What changed is that it
went from **decorative** — with one member, a player needed 125 Pastures to reach the free band
at all — to real.

| infernal | 0.50 | 4.54% | 8.33% | 16.67% | 25.00% | 33.33% | 41.67% | 45.45% |

25% of cap at 5 kills, 50% at 10, 75% at 50, 90% at 100 — and the cap is now genuinely

**Part 7 — `XP_PER_SECOND = 2`.** See §6; the constant is named, the value is UNVERIFIED, and it
turned out to be the round's biggest pacing lever.

**Part 8 — the undo re-roll is bounded, and it is asserted by outcome.** The marker lives at
module scope (`rerollPenalty`, `undoKind`), which is the one place this part could have silently
done nothing: `doUndo()` replaces `S` wholesale, so a flag inside `S` would be erased by the very
act that sets it. All 24 `Math.random` sites inside `EXPEDITIONS` route through
`rerollHit`/`rerollAmt`/`rerollFail`. Verified by **forced-fail outcome, not by reading the
flag**: best roll 57 furs → undo → **36** (the floor of the range) → 57 again (cleared after
exactly one attempt). A wrong-kind undo does not penalise. The marker survives
`serialize()` → `loadFromString()`, so saving inside the window cannot launder it.

**Discovered while implementing Part 8, and worth recording:** trades never call
`snapshotUndo()` at all. The trade half of the exploit does not exist today. The guard shipped
anyway, so it exists before the feature does rather than after.

### `test-offline-v54`'s two failures were the test reading the fixture, not the game

Both offline parity checks failed after Part 3, and the diagnosis is not what it looked like.

**"BIT-IDENTICAL" passed at v0.54 for a reason the assertion never stated: the fixture was
saturated.** Measured on the v0.54 slice, the healthy settlement sat pinned to its 4,700
provisions cap for **13,010 of 18,000 ticks** and finished pinned there. A hard clamp destroys
every trace of integration difference — both arms were being compared at `min(x, 4700)`. Exact
zero was a property of the fixture, not of catch-up.

Part 3 makes the same fixture dynamic (999 ticks at the cap, finishing at 27,364 of 47,000), and
the residual it exposes is **1.59 provisions in 27,364 = 0.0058%** — the expected artefact of a
nonlinear clamp sampled at two granularities. Re-pointed to a 0.02% tolerance **plus a new
companion assertion that the fixture is dynamic**, so it can never go vacuous again.

The starving arm is a genuine, small, knowing trade. v0.54 drifted **−7.69% on pop itself** and
had to exclude pop to pass; v0.55 gets **pop exact** and pays for it in sub-1% integrals on the
population-linear resources (worst 0.61% on renown). Agreeing on the discrete event and
disagreeing slightly on the continuous integral is the better failure mode — a returning player
counts wanderers, not decimal places of ore.

### What actually happened, in three findings

**1. s5 is not a null slice, and calling it one was the planning error of the round.** Era 3
came *back* 193.6 years, and it is entirely the late edge: Icathia y1010.8 → y837.7 (**−173.0**)
while Sparks moved +20.5. Wanderer rank is a per-trade production multiplier; doubling the
accrual rate makes every wanderer reach every rank twice as fast. The median XP bank at Icathia
went **46,905 → 176,750 (×3.77)** and the top bank 806,257 → 1,335,491. A change filed under
"experience and flavour" is the largest single accelerant the round shipped.

**2. Sparks moved 37.6 years on s2, against a ±15 diagnostic — and the diagnostic's stated cause
is ruled out.** The spec said: *if Sparks moves more than 15 years on s2, the ×10 sweep missed a
cost.* **It did not.** The sweep is thirteen sites, enumerated out of the live game after the
fact (§4), and every one is at ×10. The real cause is that **the rescale was never neutral** and
the ±15 prediction assumed it was: Part 3.3 cuts the Farmstead from ×2.24 of the source's rate
to ×1.00, so the starter food building lost **55.4% of its output relative to the economy**, and
the seasonal farmer adds a mean 6.25% cut with a brutal winter trough on top. Era 1 got harder
on purpose. The prediction, not the sweep, was wrong.

**3. s3 undershot and moved Sparks the wrong way**, which is the failure the spec asked to watch
for: *if Era 3 shortens on s3, the Petricite delay freed ore for something more valuable than the
Quarry.* Era 3 did not shorten, but Sparks came **22.3 years earlier** than s2 — the Quarry being
pushed out of reach leaves the bot's ore going into Forges and Mines instead, which is a better
Era-2 investment than the Quarry was. The hunt-yield deletion also cost less than expected: the
camp multiplier at Sparks went ×3.24 → ×5.75, i.e. **up**, because the five Discoveries are
unconditional now and the bot researches them long before it would have built 40 Lodges.
